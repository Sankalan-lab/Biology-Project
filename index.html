01 — INTRODUCTION

What are Genetic Disorders?

Genetic disorders are conditions caused by changes or abnormalities in a person's DNA or chromosomes. These changes may be inherited from parents or may occur spontaneously.

🧬

DNA Changes

Changes in the DNA sequence can affect the instructions used by cells to make proteins.

🧫

Chromosomal Changes

Extra, missing or structurally altered chromosomes can cause genetic conditions.

02 — DNA & CHROMOSOMES

The Genetic Blueprint

DNA stores biological information. It is organized into genes, which are located on chromosomes inside the cell nucleus.

DNA Double Helix

01

DNA

Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) is the molecule that contains hereditary information in living organisms.

02

Genes

A gene is a segment of DNA that contains information needed to produce a functional product, often a protein.

03

Chromosomes

Chromosomes are structures made primarily of DNA and proteins. They organize genetic material inside cells.

04

Mutation

A mutation is a change in DNA. Some mutations have little effect, while others can contribute to disease.

04 — GENETIC DISORDERS

Major Genetic Disorders

Genetic disorders can result from changes in individual genes, chromosome numbers or chromosome structure.

01
🧬

Down Syndrome

Chromosomal Disorder

Down syndrome is usually caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21, known as trisomy 21.

02
🩸

Sickle Cell Disease

Single-Gene Disorder

Sickle cell disease is caused by inherited changes in the HBB gene that affect haemoglobin.

03
🩸

Haemophilia

X-Linked Disorder

Haemophilia is an inherited disorder in which blood clotting is impaired because of reduced clotting factors.

04
🧬

Thalassemia

Blood Disorder

Thalassemia is a group of inherited disorders affecting haemoglobin production.

05
🧠

Huntington's Disease

Autosomal Dominant

Huntington's disease is caused by a mutation in the HTT gene and follows an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern.

06
🧬

Turner Syndrome

Chromosomal Disorder

Turner syndrome occurs when a female has only one complete X chromosome instead of two.

06 — INHERITANCE PATTERNS

Different Ways Disorders Can be Inherited

A

Autosomal Dominant

A disease-causing variant in one copy of a gene can be sufficient to cause the condition.

aa

Autosomal Recessive

Usually, two disease-causing copies of a gene are required for the condition to occur.

X

X-Linked

The gene responsible for the condition is located on the X chromosome.

DNA

Mitochondrial

Some genetic conditions result from variants in mitochondrial DNA.

07 — CAUSES

What Can Cause Genetic Disorders?

01

Gene Mutation

A change in the DNA sequence of a gene can alter the protein produced by that gene.

02

Chromosome Number

An individual may have an extra or missing chromosome. Down syndrome is an example of trisomy.

03

Chromosome Structure

Sections of chromosomes may be deleted, duplicated, inverted or rearranged.

04

New Mutations

Some genetic changes occur for the first time in an individual rather than being inherited from a parent.

08 — AWARENESS

Can Genetic Disorders Be Prevented?

Not all genetic disorders can be prevented. However, genetic counselling, carrier screening, prenatal testing and appropriate medical care can help families understand genetic risks and make informed healthcare decisions.

Genetic Counselling Helps individuals and families understand genetic risks.
Genetic Testing Can identify certain genetic changes.
Early Diagnosis Can help with appropriate medical management.
CONCLUSION

Genetics Helps Us Understand Life

Genetic disorders demonstrate the importance of DNA, genes and chromosomes in human biology. Understanding genetics helps scientists and healthcare professionals identify genetic conditions, study their causes and improve diagnosis and management.
